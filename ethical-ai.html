Ethical Usage of Generative AI

Demilich Digital LLC is my “Corporate Person” for commercial activity — consulting on technical and organization topics, and publishing software and creative works. That means my activities intersect with multiple types of Generative AI usage, each with very different ethical considerations.

The Framework

At its core, Generative AI is a mechanism of “intellectual property laundering.” It allows those with the economic resources to “transform” arbitrary works into a form that can be re-used without credit or compensation.

Thus the ethics of it are a function of that domain’s relationship with intellectual property, and the behavior of those using it at scale in that domain.

The four domains my work touches each land in a different place.

Artistic Assets & Prose

Artists have intrinsic value to our society in a way pure capitalism cannot value. Our cultural contract around art is about ensuring an environment where art and artists can be economically supported and sustained. Current image/asset generation models were largely trained on content without license, or license in a context well outside of its intent and in abuse of our social contract.

I do not see any ethical means of AI image generation for commercial use. Similarly, I would consider presenting AI generated images and prose in a media product as plagiarism.

I very intentionally use a simplified visual style so that my limited artistic capacity will suffice for any product I produce.

Any visual assets I did not directly create will be properly licensed and credited.

Open Source Software Engineering

Unlike art, software has a very different cultural contract regarding intellectual property. Open Source Software is critical socialized infrastructure for the modern world — so effective that despite the hyper-capitalistic nature of the tech industry it continues to see investment and thrive. Intellectual property in software is largely abusive of our commons, whereas with art it is supportive. This dramatically changes my perspective on Generative AI in this domain.

I believe the overwhelming majority of software should be open source and shared. Unlike media, the usage of source code in training models is within our cultural intent. These models offer me a unique opportunity: I can use the “laundering” nature of Generative AI to produce more Open Source content.

I not only enthusiastically use generative AI services for Open Source, I feel an obligation to do so prolifically. This tool should be used to increase the value of our Commons — which comes with a responsibility to ensure any such contributions are of high quality and reliability.

Any software produced by Demilich Digital outside the context of contracts will be Open Source and freely available.

Contract Software Engineering

My primary role is as a Software Engineer. I contract in the context of commercial software development. While I disagree with the norms of my industry, I have a legal and ethical responsibility to respect my contract with my client. I will use what power and freedom is available to me to encourage corporate contribution to the open source commons.

I use commercial LLMs for software development enthusiastically and effectively — including building infrastructure and training to do so more effectively.

Open-Weight, Locally Hosted LLMs

While I would prefer truly “Open Source” LLM models, open weight models offer a very different ethical situation than models provided as a service. They fundamentally flip the power relationship of the situation, and thus change the ethics.

Where scraping art and text to produce a model that is then used by the powerful to further disenfranchise others is theft, producing a model that enables everyone individually is a contribution to the commons. The lack of rent-extraction is a critical difference. Further, locally hosted models respect individual privacy and agency — where an integrated AI service is a surveillance and mass manipulation tool, a self-hosted tool does not further increase wealth disparity or erode the agency of individuals.

Media generated by an open model is still plagiarism, but using local LLMs as an automation, reference, and software development tool is a radically more positive ethical situation. I use local open-weight models for all use cases they are viable for, and I will continue to build tooling that enables local LLMs to contribute to society positively.

In Practice

  • Art & visual assets — no AI-generated images or prose in shipped products; anything not made in-house is properly licensed and credited.
  • Independent open source — everything built outside a client contract ships open source and freely available, produced prolifically with generative AI.
  • Client contract work — commercial LLMs used enthusiastically and effectively, within the bounds of the contract, while pushing for open source contribution wherever possible.
  • Automation & dev tooling — local, open-weight models preferred wherever viable, for privacy, agency, and a healthier balance of power.
